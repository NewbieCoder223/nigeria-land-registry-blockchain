**PROJECT FORMAT AND ARRANGEMENT**

**INTRODUCTION**

Students are advised to read this Project Report Guide carefully before beginning their final year project. This guide has been developed to help ensure that all project reports meet the approved formatting, writing, and presentation standards of the University. The document is accessible through the student portal, the University library website (under research tools), and the Academic Planning Office. Students may also visit the Academic Office or the Library for further clarification or support regarding these guidelines.

This guide is not intended to serve as a complete academic writing manual. Students are therefore encouraged to follow the American Psychological Association (APA) referencing and writing style for citation and general academic presentation. Careful attention should be given to spelling, grammar, punctuation, and clarity of expression. All project work must be original, and students must not submit work that belongs to others. For guidance on plagiarism and academic misconduct, students should consult the Student Honour Code.

In cases where there is any difference between the instructions in an external style manual and the guidelines in this document, the rules in this guide take precedence. Students are also advised not to use previous students’ projects as formatting models, as past submissions may not comply with current University standards. It is the responsibility of each student to ensure that their project is properly formatted, corrected, and approved before final submission.

All academic streams will follow the same general project format (including structure, order of sections, font type, font size, and referencing style) to ensure uniformity across the University. However, students are permitted to use discipline-specific writing formats for technical content such as calculations, equations, and formulas, using appropriate tools such as LaTeX or Microsoft Word, depending on the requirements of their programme.

SIGNATURE PAGE

**Include the title in CAPS and the student’s name.**

State: A PROJECT APPROVED BY THE [DEPARTMENT NAME].

Include space for signatures of the Supervisor, Head of Department, and Chief Academic Officer with names listed below.


ABSTRACT

Include an abstract page following these guidelines:

**Heading: ABSTRACT (all caps, centred, 2 inches from top)**

**Begin text one double-spaced line below heading.**

**Text: 150–200 words, justified, no extra spacing between lines.**

**Page number: lower-case Roman numeral (v) centred 2.5cm from bottom.**


DEDICATION, ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS, PREFACE (OPTIONAL)

Dedication: Centre text, 2 inches from top. Example: “To my family.”

Acknowledgements and Preface: Heading in all caps, centred 2.5 cm from top, text starts one double-spaced line below heading, double-spaced.

**Number pages with consecutive lower-case Roman numerals (continuing from abstract).**

TABLE OF CONTENTS

Use the following guidelines for the table of contents:

Include the heading “TABLE OF CONTENTS” in capital letters, and centre it 2.5cm below the top of the page.

Include one double-spaced line between the heading and the first entry.

The table of contents should not contain listings for the pages that precede it, but it must list all parts of the project report that follow it.

If relevant, be sure to list all appendices and a references section in your table of contents. Include page numbers for these items but do not assign separate chapter numbers.

Entries must align with the document’s left margin or be indented to the right of the left page margin using consistent tabs.

Major subheadings within chapters must be included in the table of contents. The subheading(s) should be indented to the right of the left page margin using consistent tabs.

If an entry takes up more than one line, break up the entry about three-fourths of the way across the page and place the rest of the text on a second line, single-spacing the two lines.

Page numbers listed in the table of contents must be located just inside the right page margin with leaders (lines of periods) filling out the space between the end of the entry and the page number. The last digit of each number must line up on the right margin.

Information included in the table of contents must match the headings, major subheadings, and numbering used in the body of the project report.

The Table of Contents page(s) must be numbered with consecutive lower case Roman numeral centred with a 2.5cm margin from the bottom edge.

LIST OF TABLES, FIGURES AND ILLUSTRATIONS

If applicable, include a list of tables, list of figures, and/or list of illustrations following these

Guidelines:

Include the heading(s) in all capital letters, centred 1” below the top of the page.

Include one double-spaced line between the heading and the first entry.

Each entry must include a number, title, and page number.

Assign each table, figure, or illustration in your project report an Arabic numeral. You may number consecutively throughout the entire work (e.g., Figure 1, Figure 2, etc.), or you may assign a two-part Arabic numeral with the first number designating the chapter in which it appears, separated by a period, followed by a second number to indicate its consecutive placement in the chapter (e.g., Table 3.2 is the second table in Chapter Three).

Numerals and titles must align with the document’s left margin or be indented to the right of the left page margin using consistent tabs.

Numbers, titles, and page numbers must each match the corresponding numbers, titles, and page numbers appearing in the project report.

All Lists of Tables, Figures, and Illustrations page(s) must be numbered with consecutive lower case Roman numerals centred with a 2.5cm margin from the bottom edge.


LIST OF SYMBOLS

If you use symbols in your project report, you may combine them with your abbreviations, titling the section “LIST OF ABBREVIATIONS AND SYMBOLS”, or you may set up a separate list of symbols and their definitions by following the formatting instructions:

Include the heading “LIST OF ABBREVIATIONS” in all capital letters, and centre it 1” below the top of the page.

Include one double-spaced line between the heading and the first entry.

Arrange your abbreviations alphabetically.

Abbreviations must align with the document’s left margin or be indented to the right of the left page margin using consistent tabs.

If an entry takes up more than one line, single-space between the two lines.

The List of Abbreviations page(s) must be numbered with consecutive lower case Roman numerals centred with a 2.5cm margin from the bottom edge.

**Note that the heading you choose must be in all capital letters and centred 1” below the**

top of the page.

**Margins: Left 3.5 cm, others 2.5 cm**

**Paragraphs: Indent new paragraphs; leave extra space between sections**

**Justification: Text should be justified**


III.PAGINATION

Use the following guidelines to paginate your project report:

**Preliminary pages (title, certification, abstract, etc.): lower-case Roman numerals (ii, iii, iv…)**

**Chapters and rest of document: Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3…)**

**Page numbers: centred at the bottom; do not use headers/footers**


**IV. CHAPTER-BY-CHAPTER GUIDE**

**Chapter 1: Introduction**

Context of the project (background of the study): This section should include introducing the general area of study (broad topic), then narrowing it down to the specific focus, showing the real-world or academic importance of the topic, and briefly mentioning key concepts, trends, or policies related to the topic. This is because it answers, “What is happening in this field, and where does my study fit in?”

Problem statement: clearly describes the gap, challenge, or issue in the field; shows what is not working, missing, or unknown, supported by logic, observations, or literature; ending with what the study seeks to address. It answers, “What exactly is wrong, and why does it matter?” A good problem statement is specific, not vague, researchable, and links directly to your objectives and research questions

The purpose of the study: states what your research intends to achieve. It should follow logically from the problem statement, use clear action verbs such as examine, assess, analyse, evaluate, determine, and mention the key variables, target population or context, and method (optional). It answers, “What exactly will this study do?”

Aim and objectives: The aim of your project are associated with things that you are going to - design or develop or evaluate or analyse or investigate. Examples of aim for a student project are:

To develop a database system to administer a school.

To develop a web-based system to promote a restaurant and take orders.

To investigate the use of a particular tool or technique in a particular project environment.

To design and develop a prototype app to record sightings of a predefined set of birds, their location, date on a nature reserve.

Objectives are more specific things that you need to complete in order to achieve the aim of your project. If the aim of your project is to develop a database system for a telephone company – your objectives might be:

Complete a literature search about existing database systems for telephone companies.

Gather user requirements.

Develop a model of the database system for YOUR project.

Code the model to produce a working system.

Test the working system.

Deploy the working system.

Scope of the project: a precise list of what the project will and will not deliver.

Significance: justifies why the project was necessary by highlighting its contributions to the field, practical applications, and benefits to specific stakeholders.

**Guidance on Chapter Content**

Chapter 1: Introduction

This chapter provides the context of your work in terms of:

The system developed

The solution that emerged

The main aims, objectives and scope of the project

A short overview of the remaining chapters


Chapter 2: Literature review

**Introduction – Short overview of the chapter and its purpose.**

**Review of Related Literature**

**2.1 Theme 1 – e.g., Background/Existing Systems**

**2.2 Theme 2 – e.g., Methodologies/Tools Used**

**2.3 Theme 3 – e.g., Findings from Previous Studies**

**Theoretical Framework – The theory guiding your project.**

**Conceptual Framework – Diagram or description of how your project works.**

**Research Gap – What is missing in previous studies that your project addresses.**

**Summary of Chapter – Key points, frameworks, and link to your project.**


**Chapter Three: Analysis and Design**

**1. Introduction**

Briefly explain the purpose of this chapter: to show what the system/project needs to do and how it is designed.

**2. System/Project Requirements**

**2.1 Functional Requirements – Specific features or actions the system must perform.**

2.2 Non-Functional Requirements – Performance, usability, reliability, security, or other constraints.

**3. Use Case Analysis**

**3.1 Use Case Diagrams – Show actors and interactions with the system.**

**3.2 Use Case Descriptions – Step-by-step explanation of each use case.**

**4. System/Project Architecture**

**4.1 Overview Diagram – High-level representation of the system structure.**

**4.2 Components Description – Explanation of modules or subsystems.**

**4.3 Data Flow / Interaction – How data moves between modules.**

**5. Design Specification**

**5.1 Structural Model – Class diagrams, ER diagrams, database structure, or tables.**

**5.2 Behavioral Model – Sequence diagrams, activity diagrams, or state diagrams.**

**5.3 User Interface Design (if applicable) – Screen layouts, forms, or navigation structure.**

**6. Summary of Chapter**

Recap key points of analysis and design.

**Connect to Chapter Four (Implementation).**


**Chapter Four: Implementation**

**1. Introduction**

**Explain the purpose: to show how the system/project was built.**

Mention tools, programming languages, or platforms used.

**2. Implementation Approach**

**2.1 Development Environment – Hardware, software, or IDEs used.**

**2.2 Programming Language / Tools – Language(s), frameworks, or libraries.**

**2.3 System Modules – How each part of the project was implemented.**

**3. System Installation / Setup**

Steps to install, configure, or run the system.

Include screenshots if relevant.

**4. Data Migration / Integration (if applicable)**

How existing data (if any) was transferred to the new system.

**5. Testing and Validation**

**5.1 Testing Approach – How the system was tested (unit testing, integration testing, etc.).**

**5.2 Test Cases and Results – Show sample test cases and outcomes.**

**6. User Training / Guide (if applicable)**

How users can operate the system (instructions, manuals, or brief training guide).

**7. Summary of Chapter**

Recap the key points of implementation.

**Link to Chapter Five (Conclusion and Recommendations).**


**Chapter Five: Conclusion and Recommendations**

**1. Introduction**

Brief overview of the chapter.

**2. Summary of Findings / Achievements**

**Discuss what was done and what was achieved.**

Refer back to the project objectives from Chapter One.

**3. Evaluation of Objectives**

**Evaluate how well the project met its aims and objectives.**

**4. Challenges / Limitations**

Any difficulties encountered during the project.

Technical, logistical, or resource limitations.

**5. Recommendations / Future Work**

Suggestions for improving or expanding the project.

Ideas for students or developers who may continue this work.

**6. Final Remarks**

Short concluding statement about the overall project outcome.
